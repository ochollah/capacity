Pattern finder: calibrate your base

How it works: (1) a = your base level, 0 to 100, from none to abundant. (2) b = the level of its paired height parameter that the base needs, read from the zone you are in (for example bread pairs with the Word). (3) c = √(a² + b²) is the result on the hypotenuse. (4) Your average base level sets your stage: scarcity or building (below 34) reads as the Grace foundation (3:4:5); the middle (34 to 66) reads as Warfare demolition (5:12:13); abundance (67 and above) reads as Glory (7:24:25) or Dominion (8:15:17), never as Grace. (5) Abundance is not the finish line but the trust: the more you hold, the higher the height you need, so in the abundant zone the needed height rises faster than the base (worship, will, footstool and bread lift toward Glory at 24:7; sonship, princehood, chosen men and neighbour love lift toward Dominion at 15:8). The Sabbath cycle: seven-year walk

Years 1 to 6 build: seed, root, fruit. Year 7 is a release round (Deut 15:1–2): arrears reviewed, loans forgiven or rolled over, unused surplus pooled. After seven sevens, year 50 is Jubilee: land and liberty return to families (Lev 25:8–13). Joseph stored grain in the seven good years so none starved in the lean ones (Gen 41:34–36).

Method: Commitment index CI = Σ (sector weight × share of its five actions completed). Pool T = top-10 share × base% × CI. A share k of the pool is seeded as capital and the rest is relief. Gain to the bottom 40% = T × efficiency × ((1 − k) + k × multiplier). Efficiency = base efficiency + adopted innovations × gain per innovation, capped at 99.5%. Poverty falls in proportion to how much of the gap to the “need line” the bottom 40% closes. Years to near-zero repeat the annual round, with the seventh-year boost, until the gap ratio is 1.05 or lower. Distribution is by need (Acts 4:35), so no one is forced. Honesty note: income-share defaults, sector weights, k, efficiency, multiplier and innovation gains are illustrative assumptions, not official statistics. Kenya shift economy

Idle hours are idle capital. This model turns spare machine, classroom and counter hours into four-hour paid shifts, on the same four triples, and shows jobs, poverty, growth and service effects for Kenya, with the Bible’s own rota patterns beside each step.

What the model does

Kenya runs most buildings and machines for about 40 of the 168 hours in a week. Idle hours sit beside 3.5 million idle youth and a 5% unemployment rate that hides much larger under-use. A shift pattern splits each post into several four-hour rotations. New rotations are filled first from the unemployed, then from idle youth. Their pay lifts households over the poverty line, and the extra hours raise how much of the capital stock is working.

The triangle: base = operating hours per day (counted in four-hour shifts), height = days per cycle, hypotenuse = economic stride. Because each pattern is a true Pythagorean triple (3:4:5, 5:12:13, 8:15:17, 7:24:25), the stride is always a whole number.
